Privacy English

The Grand Tour is made by Martijn, in Belgium. You can always reach him at hello@grandtourbureau.com.

Everything you play stays on this device: your progress, your records and your settings.

When you finish a round, only the score goes to our server, with a random code your browser generates. No name, no location, nothing about your phone. Scores are deleted after ninety days, and so are the backup copies we keep of them. Our server does see your internet address, as every website does: it is used for a moment to stop floods of requests, and never stored.

If you move your progress to another device, it waits on our server for ten minutes at most and is deleted as soon as the code is used.

If online backup is on, a copy of your progress is kept on our server so you can get it back if a device loses it. It is stored under a recovery code only you have, with nothing that says who you are, and deleted after ninety days without a backup, or straight away when you turn it off.

A duel runs on our server while you play: the questions, both players' answers, the two scores and each player's rating. A casual duel keeps nothing that says who you are. A ranked duel also keeps your random code and the name you picked, plus a scrambled copy of the key that proves the code is yours, so the result goes to the right rating. All of it is deleted two minutes after the last player leaves. A challenge waits up to two days for someone to play it, then goes the same way. While it waits, our server lists its six-letter code so a stranger can find it, and nothing else.

Once you pick a name or play a ranked duel, your rating is kept on our server under that same random code. That name is the only thing other players see: it appears on the leaderboard next to your rating, and your opponents see it too. You need one to play ranked; the rest of the game works the same without it. You pick a name once; write to us if you want it removed. Your rating, record and name are all deleted after ninety days without a ranked duel.

Turn notifications on and we keep the address your browser gives us for that one device. It is only used to tell you that someone has played one of your challenges, or wants revenge. The notification itself contains nothing - no game, no score, no player - the app looks that up when you open it. Turn them off and the address is deleted straight away. It is also deleted after ninety days without a ranked duel, or when your browser tells us the device is gone.

We keep two more things about ranked play. At the end of each month we record where every player finished - their rating and position - so the game can show you your season. That record is deleted after ninety days, and your phone keeps its own copy. When you ask for revenge, the challenge is kept for the other player with your name on it, so they can find it. It is deleted once it is played, or after two days.

When you open the game, Cloudflare, which hosts it, counts the visit: which page, which kind of browser, roughly which country. It uses no cookies and keeps nothing that tells us who you are. We only ever see totals.

Why? A scoreboard needs scores to compare, and that is all we use them for: our legitimate interest, kept to the least that works. Notifications are kept only because you turned them on. If you'd rather not be counted, turn off the switch above; the game works the same.

Cloudflare runs our server, our database and this website, and counts the visits. If you turn on notifications, your browser's push service (Google, Apple or Mozilla) delivers them. Cloudflare may handle data outside the EU, under the EU's standard contractual clauses and the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. We don't sell data, show ads or share it with anyone else.

Ranked play, a public name and notifications are for players aged 16 or over. If you're younger, play everything else: it all stays on your device.

You can ask what we keep under your code, have it corrected or deleted, object to it, or take a copy. Write to hello@grandtourbureau.com or use the feedback form in Settings, with your code, and we answer within a month. You can also complain to the Belgian Data Protection Authority (dataprotectionauthority.be) or the one in your own country. Nothing about you is decided by a machine.

Überträgst du deinen Fortschritt auf ein anderes Gerät, wartet er höchstens zehn Minuten auf unserem Server und wird gelöscht, sobald der Code benutzt ist.

Ist die Online-Sicherung an, liegt eine Kopie deines Fortschritts auf unserem Server, damit du ihn zurückholen kannst, wenn ein Gerät ihn verliert. Sie liegt unter einem Wiederherstellungscode, den nur du hast, ohne etwas, das sagt, wer du bist, und wird nach neunzig Tagen ohne Sicherung gelöscht, oder sofort, wenn du sie ausschaltest.

Ein Duell läuft auf unserem Server, solange ihr spielt: die Fragen, beide Antworten, beide Punktestände und beide Wertungen. Ein lockeres Duell speichert nichts, was verrät, wer ihr seid. Ein gewertetes Duell speichert außerdem deinen Zufallscode, eine verschlüsselte Form des Schlüssels, der beweist, dass er dir gehört - damit das Ergebnis bei der richtigen Wertung landet - und deinen Namen. Zwei Minuten nachdem der Letzte gegangen ist, ist alles gelöscht. Eine Herausforderung wartet höchstens zwei Tage auf eine Antwort und ist dann ebenso weg. Solange sie wartet, steht ihr sechsstelliger Code auf einer Liste, damit ein Fremder sie findet - sonst nichts.

Sobald du einen Namen wählst oder ein gewertetes Duell spielst, bewahrt unser Server deine Wertung unter demselben zufälligen Code auf. Der Name ist das Einzige in diesem Spiel, das andere zu sehen bekommen: Er steht in der Rangliste neben deiner Wertung, und wer gegen dich spielt, sieht ihn ebenfalls. Für gewertete Duelle brauchst du einen; der Rest des Spiels geht auch ohne. Ein Name wird einmal gewählt; schreib uns, und er wird wieder entfernt. Wertung, Bilanz und Name werden nach neunzig Tagen ohne gewertetes Duell zusammen gelöscht.

Am Ende jedes Monats notieren wir, wo alle gelandet sind, die gespielt haben - Wertung und Platz -, damit dir das Spiel deine Saison zeigen kann.
